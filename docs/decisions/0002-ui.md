# 0002. UI of the bike bundle configurator

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-15
- **Covers:** decisions U1 to U11. Cite them by number in tasks and pull requests.
- **Related:** [0001. Architecture of the bike bundle configurator](0001-architecture.md) (decisions A1 to A14), `AGENTS.md`, [`docs/brief.md`](../brief.md)

This record decides how the configurator, the cart and the order result look and behave. Terms (bundle, cart line, cart) and the server-side decisions it refers to are in [0001](0001-architecture.md).

U1, U2, U3, U9 and U10 chose between competing designs. U4 to U8 and U11 mostly specify behaviour; where no competing design was weighed, the entry says so.

## Screens and flows

1. **Configurator `/`.** Pick a bike → compatible accessories become adjustable → set quantities → the summary shows net, VAT and gross → "Add to cart" → a notice confirms and offers "View cart"; the header badge counts the bundles.
2. **Cart `/cart`.** One card per bundle with its positions and prices → remove a bundle, or clear the cart → totals → "Place order".
3. **Order result.** Confirmed, shortfall, or stock unavailable (U9).

The data has no stock field; stock numbers come from the simulated stock service (A8).

## U1. Visual direction

**Decision.** "Workshop": white and zinc surfaces, near-black type, one blue accent, 1 px borders, small radius.

**Context.** The three directions are three token sets; the components and the build cost are identical. None of them imitates BIKE24's branding.

**Options considered.**

| Option          | Look                                                                                  | For                                                                                                    | Against                                                                                    |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **A. Workshop** | White and zinc surfaces, near-black type, one blue accent, 1 px borders, small radius | Highest contrast. Stock and error colours (green, amber, red) stay unambiguous against neutral chrome. | Least personality                                                                          |
| B. Trailhead    | Warm off-white, ink type, forest-green accent, softer radius                          | Friendly, outdoor-retail feel                                                                          | The green accent sits close to the "in stock" green                                        |
| C. Night ride   | Dark slate, high-visibility lime accent                                               | Most distinctive                                                                                       | Hardest to keep contrast right across every state; disabled items are harder to tell apart |

**Reason.** The states (stock, errors, disabled items) are what the brief judges; "Workshop" makes them the loudest thing on screen. Because the direction is a token set (U10), changing it later is a token change.

## U2. Configurator layout

**Decision.** One page with two columns: bikes on the left; accessories and a sticky bundle summary on the right.

**Context.** The configurator has to offer bike selection, compatible accessories, quantities and the bundle price with "Add to cart" (requirements 1 to 4).

**Options considered.**

| Option                                                                           | For                                                                                                                                                                            | Against                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **a. One page, two columns: bikes left; accessories and a sticky summary right** | Everything the brief names is visible at once. Changing the bike updates the accessories in place, which demonstrates compatibility immediately. One route, one client island. | Dense on a phone (handled by stacking, U11)                                             |
| b. Three-step wizard: bike → accessories → review                                | Calm, focused steps                                                                                                                                                            | More state and navigation to build and test; hides the compatibility rule behind a step |
| c. Bike grid, then a page per bike                                               | Shareable URL per bike; the server filters accessories                                                                                                                         | Two routes; a request per bike; more to build for the same requirement                  |

**Reason.** One page shows the compatibility rule at work the moment a bike is chosen, and it needs one route and one client island (A5).

## U3. Incompatible accessories

**Decision.** Incompatible accessories are listed in their own group at the end, under a heading such as "Not compatible with Road frames". The group is expanded by default. Its rows are dimmed and disabled, have no slider, and state the frame types they do fit.

**Context.** Requirement 2: incompatible accessories "must not be selectable". For a Road bike, 8 of the 16 accessories are incompatible.

**Options considered.**

| Option                                                                                 | For                                                                | Against                                                                                             |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| a. Shown in catalog order, disabled in place                                           | The rule is visible                                                | Half the list is dead rows between live ones                                                        |
| b. Hidden                                                                              | Cleanest list                                                      | The rule is invisible: nobody can see that it was implemented, and customers cannot see what exists |
| **c. Grouped at the end under "Not compatible with Road frames", disabled, no slider** | The rule is visible and explained, and the usable list stays short | Slightly longer page                                                                                |

**Reason.** The rule stays visible and comes with its explanation, while the accessories the customer can use stay together at the top.

## U4. Bike selection and quantity slider

**Decision.**

- **Bike selection.** A radio group rendered as cards: name, frame type, gross price, stock badge. States: default, hover, keyboard focus (visible ring), selected (accent border and a check mark), out of stock (selectable, flagged). Nothing is preselected; the accessory panel says "Select a bike to see matching accessories".
- **Quantity.** A native `<input type="range">` from 0 to `maxAmount`, step 1, with tick marks and the current value shown as "2 of 4". Zero means "not in the bundle". States: empty (0), partial, at maximum, focus, disabled (no bike selected).
- **Changing the bike** keeps the quantities of accessories that are still compatible and drops the rest.

**Context.** Requirement 1 asks for selecting a bike from a list, requirement 3 for setting a quantity "via a slider, limited by its `maxAmount`".

**Options considered.** One alternative was weighed: a different control for accessories whose `maxAmount` is 1. Not chosen: such an accessory still gets a slider with two stops, because the requirement says slider and one control means one keyboard model.

**Reason.** Native radio inputs and range inputs give keyboard operation without extra code (U10, U11), and one quantity control behaves the same for every accessory.

## U5. Stock display

**Decision.**

| State                | Shown                                                      |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Loading              | skeleton badge                                             |
| More than 5          | "In stock" (green)                                         |
| 1 to 5               | "Only 3 left" (amber)                                      |
| 0                    | "Out of stock" (red)                                       |
| Stock service failed | "Stock unknown" (grey) and one "Retry" for the whole panel |

Rule: **a known shortfall blocks "Add to cart"; unknown stock never does.** If the selected bike is out of stock, or a quantity exceeds the known count, "Add to cart" is disabled and the summary says what to change. If stock is unknown, "Add to cart" stays enabled with the note "We'll confirm stock at checkout".

**Context.** Availability is a hint fetched by the configurator after it loads (A10), from a service that is slow and fails about one time in ten (A8). The reservation at checkout is the decision (A7, A9).

**Options considered.** No competing design was weighed for the badges and thresholds. For the rule, blocking "Add to cart" while stock is unknown was ruled out by A9: a failed availability read never blocks configuring or adding.

**Reason.** Blocking on a known shortfall is a courtesy drawn from the hint; the server still decides at checkout. Not blocking on unknown stock keeps the unstable service from standing between the customer and the cart.

## U6. Prices

**Decision.**

- Bike cards and accessory rows lead with the **gross** price and "incl. 19 % VAT"; the net price sits beside it, smaller.
- The summary shows Net, VAT and Total (gross), with the total emphasised.
- English copy. Euro amounts are formatted as `€1,436.09` through one `formatMoney(cents)` in `ui/`.

**Context.** Requirement 4 asks for the bundle "with net and gross price (via `taxRate`)". Catalog prices are net; the server computes all amounts in cents (A12) and formatting is the UI's job (A6).

**Options considered.** No competing design was weighed. A German and English language switch is the i18n bonus, which is deferred (A13).

**Reason.** Both prices the brief asks for are visible wherever a price appears, and a single formatter keeps every amount in the same form.

## U7. "Add to cart" feedback

**Decision.** The button shows a pending state and cannot be pressed twice. On success the customer stays on the configurator, a notice in the summary says "Bundle added to your cart" with a "View cart" link, the header badge updates, and the builder resets (no bike selected). On a validation error the notice states it and the bundle is kept. The notice clears on the next interaction and when the page is hidden.

**Context.** Adding sends the bundle to the server once (A6); the badge is rendered by the server and refreshed after the write (A2). "Add to cart" does not consult the stock service (A7), so its only expected failures are validation errors.

**Options considered.** No competing design was weighed. Responding before the server answers is the Optimistic UI bonus, which is deferred (A13).

**Reason.** A button that cannot be pressed twice while pending keeps a double click from adding the bundle twice, and the bundle is kept on an error so nothing has to be configured again.

## U8. Cart screen

**Decision.**

- One card per bundle: the bike, then each accessory with quantity and net amount; line net, VAT and gross.
- **Remove** acts immediately with a pending state and no confirmation.
- **Clear cart** asks once, inline: "Clear all 3 bundles?" with "Clear cart" and "Keep".
- Totals: net, VAT, total. **Place order** below.
- Empty state: "Your cart is empty" with a link to the configurator.

**Context.** Requirement 5: view the cart and delete individual items or the entire cart. A cart line is one bundle and is removed as one unit (see Terms in [0001](0001-architecture.md)). Lines and totals are priced and rendered on the server (A5).

**Options considered.** A confirmation before removing a single bundle was weighed and not chosen, because one bundle is cheap to rebuild. Clearing the whole cart does ask, once and inline.

**Reason.** The confirmation step is spent only where the loss is large.

## U9. Order result and error messages

**Decision.** The result is shown inline on the cart page: a result panel replaces the cart. The messages:

| Outcome               | Message                                                                                                                                | Cart                             | Next action                                 |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Confirmed             | "Order confirmed. Order number ORD-7F3K2A. Total €6,595.69." (example values)                                                          | emptied                          | "Configure another bike"                    |
| Stock unavailable     | "We couldn't reach the stock service, so your order was not placed. Your cart is unchanged."                                           | intact                           | "Try again"                                 |
| Shortfall             | "Some items are no longer available in the quantity you asked for." Then each item: "Urban Pro Bike Helmet: 2 requested, 1 available." | intact; affected bundles flagged | Remove the flagged bundle, then order again |
| Network or unexpected | "Something went wrong and your order was not placed."                                                                                  | intact                           | "Try again"                                 |

**Context.** Requirement 7: a clear success or error message when completing an order, "including the case where the stock service is currently failing". Checkout has three server outcomes (A6): confirmed (200), shortfall (409, error code `shortfall`, the items in `details.items`) and stock unavailable (503, error code `stock-unavailable`). The panel switches on the HTTP status and the error code, never on message text.

**Options considered.**

| Option                                                           | For                                                                         | Against                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **a. Inline on the cart page: a result panel replaces the cart** | No new route or port. Errors and success appear where the action was taken. | The confirmation is gone after a reload                                       |
| b. Dedicated page `/order/{id}`                                  | A real, reloadable confirmation                                             | Needs an order store (A14), about one more hour                               |
| c. Modal dialog                                                  | Focused                                                                     | Focus trapping to build and test; errors in modals are easy to dismiss unread |

**Reason.** The outcome appears where the customer acted, with no new route and no order store. The stock-unavailable message says what happened, what did not happen, and that nothing was lost. The cost, a confirmation that does not survive a reload, is stated in the README (A14).

## U10. Design-system primitives on Tailwind

**Decision.** Hand-written primitives on native elements, with variants as typed maps.

- **Tokens** live in `globals.css` under `@theme`, named by role: `surface`, `surface-muted`, `line`, `ink`, `ink-muted`, `primary`, `on-primary`, and `ok`, `warn`, `danger`, each with a soft background. Components use `bg-surface`, `text-ink`, `border-line`; there are no raw palette colours in components. Changing the visual direction is a token change.
- **Primitives:** `Button` (primary, secondary, danger, ghost; pending), `Card`, `RadioCard`, `Slider`, `Badge`, `Alert`, `Price`, `Skeleton`, `VisuallyHidden`.
- **Variants** are a typed object from variant name to class string, joined by a five-line `cx` helper.
- **Rules:** primitives take no domain types; every interactive primitive has a visible focus ring; disabled is the real `disabled` attribute.

**Context.** The project uses Tailwind v4, which is configured in CSS: `globals.css` holds the tokens in `@theme` and there is no `tailwind.config.js`. `src/ui/` holds primitives with no business logic. The brief asks that every library can be justified.

**Options considered.**

| Option                                                                    | For                                                                                                                                                                  | Against                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **a. Hand-written primitives on native elements, variants as typed maps** | No dependency to justify. Native `<input type="range">`, radio inputs and `<button>` give keyboard support for free, which is the A11y bonus. About ten small files. | We write the class lists ourselves                                  |
| b. shadcn/ui (Radix)                                                      | Polished, accessible                                                                                                                                                 | Many generated files and dependencies to explain for ten components |
| c. `cva` + `tailwind-merge`                                               | Tidy variant API                                                                                                                                                     | Two libraries for a problem this size does not have                 |
| d. DaisyUI or similar                                                     | Fast                                                                                                                                                                 | The look is the library's, not ours                                 |

**Reason.** Ten components do not need a library, and native controls bring the keyboard behaviour the A11y bonus asks for.

## U11. Responsive behaviour and accessibility

**Decision.**

- **Phone.** One column: bikes, then accessories, with the summary as a bar fixed to the bottom showing the total and "Add to cart".
- **Keyboard.** Arrow keys move between bikes (radio group) and change a slider; Tab order follows the page.
- **Announcements.** The bundle total and the cart total sit in an `aria-live="polite"` region. Error alerts use `role="alert"`; success notices use `role="status"`.
- **Sliders** expose "2 of 4" as `aria-valuetext` and are labelled by the accessory name.
- **Disabled accessories** keep their reason as visible text, so it is not lost with the disabled control.
- Colour is never the only signal: every stock state has words.

**Context.** The A11y bonus is built (A13): the slider operable by keyboard and the cart total announced via `aria-live`. The two-column layout (U2) is dense on a phone.

**Options considered.** No competing design was weighed. These points follow from the layout (U2), the native controls (U4, U10) and the stock states (U5).

**Reason.** The primitives are native controls, so keyboard operation comes with them; the remaining work is labelling, live regions and making sure no state is carried by colour alone.

## Deferred to a later iteration

The full list, with reasons, is in [0001](0001-architecture.md#deferred-to-a-later-iteration). What it means for the UI:

- **Optimistic UI.** "Add to cart" waits for the server and shows a pending state (U7); it does not respond before the server answers.
- **Limit of 10 product types.** There is no limit and no progress indicator.
- **i18n.** English copy only, with no language switch (U6).
